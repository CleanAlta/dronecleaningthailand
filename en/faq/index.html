FAQ

Drone building cleaning in Thailand: the questions people ask us

Short answers, written by the people who do the work. Where a figure comes from a real job or a published rule, we say so. Where we do not know yet, we say that too.

Cost and speed

How much does drone façade cleaning cost in Thailand?

We do not publish a price per square metre, because the price depends on height, access, surface type, dirt level and how often you clean. We quote after a site survey. For the first Thai sites, the pilot program offers a tailored price in exchange for documenting the job. As a rule, drone cleaning is competitive against rope access and gondola work as soon as the building is tall, large or hard to access, because there is no scaffolding, no anchors and far fewer hours at height.

How fast is it?

500 to 800 m² per hour on suitable surfaces, depending on dirt level, method and protocol. This is the rate we use on French jobs. Set-up, safety perimeter and finishing with the pole or platform come on top. For reference, RATP reports that our night job at Paris Saint-Lazare cut intervention time by five compared with its usual method.

How often should a building be cleaned in Thailand?

More often than in Europe. Humidity, heat, dust, traffic and sea air dirty façades fast. Hotels and offices typically want glass cleaned two to four times a year. We recommend a schedule after the first job, once we have seen how fast your building gets dirty.

Is there a minimum job size?

Not a fixed one. Mobilising a drone team makes sense from a few hundred square metres upwards, or for smaller surfaces that are very hard to reach. Below that, a water-fed pole is often the right tool and we will say so.

Legal and safety

Is drone cleaning legal in Thailand?

Yes, within a framework. Drones must be registered with the NBTC and with the CAAT through the UAS Portal, the operator needs third-party liability insurance of at least 1,000,000 THB, flights are in daylight within visual line of sight, special approval is needed above 90 m, within 9 km of an airport, and over cities or gatherings. Our summary and sources are on the compliance page.

Is CleanAlta registered in Thailand today?

No. We are selecting pilot sites and a local partner. Drones, pilots, insurance and permissions will be in place before the first Thai job. We will not fly before that.

What happens if the drone fails in flight?

The drone is tethered: its cable and hose hold it, and its power comes from the ground, so it cannot fly away or run out of battery. The ground under the work zone is closed and controlled during the whole job. In France we fly under EU rules with prefecture authorisations on sensitive urban sites, including hospitals.

Who is on the ground during a job?

A pilot, a ground operator who manages the hose, the tether and the perimeter, and a pole or platform operator when needed. Nobody works at height during application.

Do you need to close the street?

Not always. We need a controlled area under the drone: a courtyard, a section of car park or a pavement closed for a few hours. We agree it with the site and, when required, with the authorities.

Method and products

What water do you use?

Osmosed water, heated, at low pressure. Reverse osmosis removes the minerals that leave white marks when tap water dries. Heat lifts grease and pollution. We bring our own treatment unit and connect to a water point on site.

Do you use detergent or chemicals?

Not on glass. On roofs, we apply slow-acting biocide or enzymatic treatments against moss and algae, chosen for the surface and used according to the label, with protection of pools, tanks and plants.

Will it leave streaks on the windows?

No. Streaks come from minerals in tap water and from detergent residues. We use neither on glass.

Can the drone damage the façade?

The drone never touches the building. It sprays at low pressure from a short distance. On sensitive materials we test a small area first and adjust temperature, distance and passes. In France we have written protocols per material: limestone, flamed stoneware, concrete, lacquered steel, glass.

What does the drone not do well?

Heavy encrusted dirt that needs brushing, very narrow spaces, and detail work on frames and joints. For those we use the water-fed pole or the aerial platform in the same visit. We also do not fly in strong wind or rain.

Buildings and weather

Which buildings are a good fit?

Buildings from about four floors up with large glass or cladding surfaces, curved façades, atriums, closed courtyards, and roofs or solar arrays that are hard to reach. See the pages for hotels, condominiums and offices, hospitals and universities and factories and solar.

Can you work while the building is occupied?

Yes. In France we clean hospitals, research centres and stations while they stay open, sometimes at night. We agree the schedule and the perimeter with the facility team.

What about the monsoon?

We do not fly in rain or strong wind. In Thailand we plan jobs in the dry season where possible and keep flexible dates during the rainy months. Early mornings are usually the calmest.

Can you clean solar panels on a factory roof?

Yes, with purified water, no detergent and no contact with the modules. We never put weight on the panels and we follow the panel manufacturer's cleaning guidelines.

Do you also inspect buildings or make 3D models?

No. The Thai offer is cleaning only. If you need inspection, thermography or 3D modelling, we can point you to specialised operators.
