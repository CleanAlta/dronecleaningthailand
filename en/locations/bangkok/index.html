Bangkok · Pilot sites wanted

Drone façade and window cleaning in Bangkok

Bangkok has more glass towers, condominiums, hospitals and malls than any other Thai city, and the dirtiest air to go with them. It is also the most regulated airspace in the country. We are looking for two or three Bangkok buildings to run our first documented jobs, with permits handled properly.

Apply as a Bangkok pilot site WhatsApp

Why Bangkok façades get dirty so fast

Traffic soot, construction dust, humidity above 70% most of the year and a long rainy season leave a grey film on glass and a black streak under every sill. Dark algae grows on north-facing and shaded walls. Buildings along the expressways and the Chao Phraya get it worst.

Most towers were built with a gondola, but many gondolas are ageing, booked, or do not reach set-backs, podiums, canopies and atriums. Rope access fills the gap, with technicians hanging outside offices and condo units for days.

A tethered drone cleans the same glass from a short distance, at 500 to 800 m² per hour on suitable surfaces, with the pilot on the ground and nobody at height during application. For podiums, canopies and inner courtyards, it is often the only tool that fits.

Airspace and permits in Bangkok

Bangkok has two airports, Suvarnabhumi to the east and Don Mueang to the north. Thai rules require permission to fly within 9 km of an airport, and urban flights need authorisation in any case. In practice, every Bangkok job needs a specific permission from the CAAT, and sites close to either airport need more lead time. We check the location of your building against these zones in the remote assessment, before anyone travels.

The 50 m rule for drones between 2 and 25 kg means we need a controlled ground area under the work zone: a service yard, a section of car park, a closed drop-off lane. Dense sites can still work, with early-morning or night slots agreed with the building.

CleanAlta has not yet registered drones or pilots in Thailand. Registrations, insurance and permissions will be done with our local partner before the first Bangkok job. Sources and details: compliance page.

What a Bangkok pilot job would look like

  1. Remote assessment

    Photos, address, height. We check airspace, take-off options and surfaces, and say whether it is realistic.

  2. Site visit with our partner

    Water and power points, perimeter, timing with building management, security briefing.

  3. Permits and insurance

    CAAT permission for the site, NBTC and CAAT registrations, insurance certificate, method statement shared with your team.

  4. Job and report

    Test area, drone on the volume, pole on the details, before-and-after photos. Then a proposal for a yearly schedule.

Bangkok questions

Do you already have a team in Bangkok?

Not yet. We are choosing a local partner and training will follow. The first jobs will be run by a CleanAlta pilot from France with the partner's team.

Can you fly near Suvarnabhumi or Don Mueang?

Only with CAAT permission, and with more lead time. Tell us the address and we will say quickly whether it is realistic.

Can you work at night in Bangkok?

Thai rules allow daylight flights only, unless a special permission is granted. In France we have done night jobs for RATP; in Thailand we will plan early mornings unless a permission allows otherwise.

How do I apply?

Through the pilot program or directly on WhatsApp with photos and the address.
