High-rise window cleaning in Bangkok: gondola, rope access or drone?

Bangkok adds glass towers every year along Sukhumvit, Sathorn, Silom, Ratchada and Rama 9. Most are cleaned by gondola or rope access. This guide explains what each method does well, where it fails, and where a tethered drone changes the equation.

By Vincent Goleau, founder of CleanAlta, professional drone pilot since 2014Published 31 October 2026Facts checked against sources linked in the text

Short answer: if your tower has a working building maintenance unit (gondola) that reaches every façade, keep using it for the main walls. Use a drone for what the gondola cannot reach: podiums, canopies, glass roofs, atriums and setbacks. If the tower has no gondola, or the gondola is out of service, a tethered drone cleans the glass at 500 to 800 m² per hour with nobody hanging in front of the units, which matters to residents and hotel guests.

How Bangkok towers are cleaned today

Three methods dominate. The building maintenance unit, a cradle on a roof rail, is standard on office towers and recent condominiums. Rope access, with technicians abseiling down the façade, is common on older buildings and on anything the BMU cannot reach. Traditional cleaning companies also use boom lifts and, for low buildings, water-fed poles.

Each has a weak point. The BMU is slow, needs maintenance, and is blind to podiums and canopies. Rope access puts people at height for days, requires certified anchors, and residents do not like a technician outside their window. Boom lifts need ground space and stop at about 40 m.

Where the drone fits

  • Towers without a working BMU. Many condominiums built before 2010, and some newer ones, have no gondola or one that nobody maintains. The drone replaces rope access on the main façades.
  • Podiums, canopies and glass roofs. The retail podium, the porte-cochère canopy and the lobby skylight are usually dirtier than the tower because no method reaches them easily. A drone does.
  • Hotels. Nobody outside the guest rooms, work done before breakfast, no cradle noise.
  • Hospitals and campuses. See how we did it at a working Paris hospital.

Where the drone does not fit

A tower with balconies on every floor and furniture on them; a façade on a narrow soi with no ground space for the van and perimeter; a site inside a no-fly zone near an airport without a specific permit. In those cases we propose rope access or a pole, or we decline.

Rules in Bangkok

A professional drone operation in Thailand needs NBTC registration of the aircraft, CAAT registration of the operator, third-party insurance of at least 1,000,000 THB, and a permission for flights in urban areas or near people. Parts of Bangkok are inside airport control zones, where flights need additional approval. CleanAlta handles this before any pilot job; details on the compliance page.

What to do with this

If you manage a tower in Bangkok, send us the address, the height, whether it has a BMU, and photos of the podium and canopies. We will tell you honestly which surfaces a drone would clean better, and which should stay with your current contractor.

Questions from Bangkok building managers

Can a drone fly between towers in Sathorn or Sukhumvit?

Yes, as long as there is a safe ground perimeter under the flight path and the site is not in a restricted zone without permit. The tether keeps the drone within a few metres of the façade.

Will residents see a drone outside their window?

Briefly, a few metres away, during the pass on their floor. Unlike rope access, there is no person outside the window and no equipment left on the façade.

Do you work at night in Bangkok?

Night work is possible on sites that need it, as we did at Paris Saint-Lazare. It requires lighting on the façade and a specific permission.
