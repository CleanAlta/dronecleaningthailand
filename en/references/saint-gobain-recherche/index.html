Saint-Gobain Recherche: from a first job to a two-passes-a-year contract

Saint-Gobain is the world’s leading glass manufacturer. Its research centre near Paris has large glazed façades over several floors and laboratories that keep working during cleaning. After a first job, Saint-Gobain Recherche signed a contract for two cleaning passes a year.

By Vincent Goleau, founder of CleanAlta, professional drone pilot since 2014Published 31 October 2026Reviewed for the Thai market

In short: tethered drone for the upper floors, water-fed pole for the lower floors and the entrance, heated purified water and no detergent. Laboratories stayed open. The first job convinced Saint-Gobain Recherche to sign a contract for two cleaning passes a year.

  • ClientSaint-Gobain Recherche
  • SiteResearch centre, Aubervilliers (Paris region)
  • MethodsDrone + water-fed pole
  • WaterHeated, osmosed, no detergent
  • OutcomeTwo passes a year

The problem

Glass façades over several floors, offices and laboratories behind them, and a site that cannot stop for cleaning. A gondola would have been slow; rope access would have put technicians in front of the windows for days.

What CleanAlta did

We combined two methods. A tethered drone cleaned the upper floors, spraying heated purified water at low pressure and rinsing it. A 17 m carbon water-fed pole handled the lower floors and the entrance, where precision matters more than speed. Both use the same water, produced on site by the osmosis unit in the van, so the glass dries without streaks and without detergent.

The result

The first job was a test. The client measured the result, compared it with its previous contractor, and signed for two passes a year. For a cleaning company, a recurring contract with a glass manufacturer is the best possible reference on the quality of the result.

Why it matters for Thailand

Office campuses, research centres and factories with glazed office blocks are common in Bangkok, the EEC and the industrial estates. The combination of drone and pole is the one we would use there: fast on the upper floors, precise at ground level, and no interruption for the people working inside.

Questions about this job

Why no detergent?

Heated osmosed water dissolves dirt and dries without leaving minerals, so there is nothing to rinse off and no residue on the glass. It is also safer for the people and plants below.

Why two methods on one building?

The drone is fastest on large upper surfaces; the pole is more precise near the ground and the entrance. Using both gives the best result for the lowest cost.
